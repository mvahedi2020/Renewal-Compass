# Renewal Compass — protect the outcome, bound the promise

An enterprise account request can look urgent and specific while the business need remains underspecified. This fictional prototype asks an account lead and product manager to choose what outcome to support without consuming the wider product roadmap or promising work whose conditions are unresolved.

Mo owns Product / Program Management framing and tradeoff direction. AI assists implementation and verification. The project uses original fictional data, with no employer contract, customer name, contract value, renewal result or endorsement. No human research has been conducted.

## Main product choice

The Vale Studio fixture needs a cross-team review with less manual file reconciliation. It asks for a custom dashboard, export and faster preparation. The sample obligation covers existing workspace access and standard support; none of those new features is already owed. A four-record evidence ledger makes the difference between an invented observed workflow and an unverified assumption visible.

The working recommendation is a shared export plus operating bridge, subject to discovery, capacity approval and bridge-owner agreement. At baseline, 14 total team days minus the six-day shared accessibility milestone leaves eight days. Export six plus bridge two uses all eight. This package might help 12 of 20 fictional accounts whose review fields overlap; it has no evidence of actual adoption or renewal influence.

The principal rejected alternative is a 14-day account-specific dashboard. It exceeds baseline package capacity by six days. Allocating the whole cycle to it would displace a shared milestone serving 12 fictional accounts. The bridge-only alternative costs two days and leaves six, but retains manual burden. The comparison presents these opportunity costs directly instead of turning invented retention value into a score.

## Responsible review, not an implied promise

The user prepares a conditional brief with an owner, review date, scope, exclusions, evidence and unresolved risks. Preview exposes the full scope before confirmation. Cancellation and Escape create no saved record. A confirmed decision is labelled conditional review; no message is sent and no delivery date is promised.

Each saved record contains exact copies of the option and evidence version. Changing a later draft does not mutate a prior review. Withdrawal preserves the original record. Restoring an earlier scope creates a new working draft that needs another review. A self-contained export preserves the same fictional boundary, scope and conditions outside the browser.

## Meaningful failure and recovery

Selecting the custom dashboard at baseline blocks decision recording. Choosing bridge-only restores feasibility without inventing new capacity. A separate state failure shows why review must bind the content being reviewed: if another tab changes saved bytes, even at the same revision number, confirmation rejects the stale action. Invalid saved content is preserved until reviewed reset. If reading saved content fails while writing would work, the app keeps decisions in memory and never overwrites unseen data.

## What the prototype demonstrates

It demonstrates a product decision workflow, calculable opportunity costs and deliberate local review/recovery. Automated checks cover the fictional arithmetic, snapshot provenance, schema/history constraints and production browser journeys. They do not establish customer acceptance, commercial impact or user comprehension. The proposed evaluation asks account leads and product managers to distinguish need from request, explain the rejected option and identify what must be true before commitment.

A real organization would need discovery, governance and explicit authority before making any commitment. Those dependencies remain visible in the prototype and are not represented as completed work.
